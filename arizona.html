Arizona's 2-Year Window to Claim Your Surplus Funds

Under A.R.S. § 33-812, excess proceeds from a trustee's sale are deposited with the county treasurer. If no claim is filed within two years of that deposit, the funds are presumed abandoned and turned over to the Arizona Department of Revenue's unclaimed property program, where recovering them gets slower and harder. The sooner you act, the simpler your claim.

What Is Surplus Fund Recovery?

When a property is sold at a foreclosure (trustee's) sale for more than what was owed, the extra money — called surplus funds or excess proceeds — is deposited with the county treasurer. Most people never know this money exists.

At Rightful Returns Recovery, we specialize in tracking down these funds across all of Arizona — from Maricopa to Mohave County — and reuniting them with their rightful owners.

There is no cost to you upfront. We work on contingency — we only earn a fee if we successfully recover money for you.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Are surplus funds legitimate?

Yes. Arizona law (A.R.S. § 33-812) allows former property owners to claim excess proceeds after a foreclosure sale. These are real funds held by the county treasurer and released by order of the Superior Court.

How long does the process take?

Timelines vary by county but typically range from several weeks to a few months. We keep you updated throughout the entire process.

What if no funds are found?

There is no obligation and no fee. Our search is completely free and risk-free for you.

Which Arizona counties do you cover?

We cover all 15 Arizona counties, including Maricopa, Pima, Pinal, Yavapai, Mohave, Yuma, Coconino, and more.
