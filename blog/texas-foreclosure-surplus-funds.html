If you or someone you know lost a home in Texas to a foreclosure or a tax sale, there is a real chance money is still waiting to be claimed. This guide explains how Texas surplus funds work, why the deadline depends on the type of sale, which counties see the most activity, and what to do next.

What Are Texas Foreclosure Surplus Funds?

When a Texas property is sold at a foreclosure auction or tax sale for more than what was owed — the mortgage balance or tax debt, costs, and liens — the money left over is called surplus funds or excess proceeds. After any junior liens are paid, that money belongs to the former property owner (or their heirs), not the county, not the lender, and not the auction winner.

What many websites get wrong is that Texas has two very different kinds of foreclosure, and the rules for claiming surplus are not the same.

Tax Sale vs. Lender Foreclosure: Two Different Sets of Rules

⚠️ Tax Sale Deadline

For tax sales, Texas Tax Code §34.04 sets a strict 2-year window to file your claim. Courts treat it as a hard cutoff. Once it passes, your right to the surplus is lost — regardless of how much money is involved.

✅ Lender Foreclosure: Don't Assume It's Too Late

Texas has no excess-proceeds statute for lender foreclosures, and the §34.04 two-year deadline does not apply to them. The trustee who ran the sale holds the surplus and has a duty to pay it to the people entitled to it.

That means if you lost a home to a lender foreclosure more than two years ago, your surplus may still be recoverable. Many former homeowners are wrongly told it's too late.

How the Texas Surplus Funds Process Works

If your home was sold at a tax sale

1

Property Sold at a Constable or Sheriff's Sale

After a court judgment for unpaid taxes, the property is auctioned at the county courthouse. The highest bidder wins.

2

Taxes, Costs, and Liens Are Paid

Sale proceeds pay the taxes, penalties, and court costs owed. Whatever is left is excess proceeds.

3

Surplus Is Deposited With the Court Clerk

The excess is held by the clerk of the court that ordered the sale. A notice may be mailed to your last known address, but if you've moved, it may never reach you.

4

File a Petition Within 2 Years

The former owner (or their authorized representative) files a petition in the court that ordered the sale before the §34.04 deadline expires.

5

Court Approves and Orders Payment

The court confirms who is entitled, usually based on proof of ownership at the time of sale, and orders the clerk to release the funds.

If your home was sold by your lender (trustee's sale)

1

Property Sold at a Trustee's Sale

The lender's trustee auctions the home at the county courthouse, typically on the first Tuesday of the month, without a court case.

2

Lender Debt and Sale Costs Are Paid

The proceeds pay the lender's debt, allowed trustee fees and costs, then any junior liens in priority order.

3

The Trustee Holds the Surplus

Anything left over is held by the trustee — often the lender's law firm — until the rightful owner or heirs are identified.

4

Submit Your Claim and Documents

The claimant provides proof of identity and entitlement. For an estate, that usually includes heirship or probate documents, and the trustee may ask for signed, notarized indemnity agreements.

5

The Trustee Issues Payment

Once the trustee accepts the claim, it issues payment to the claimants. If there are competing claims, the matter may go to court.

Why Most Texas Surplus Funds Go Unclaimed

The Texas government is not in the business of handing you a check. Notices go to your last known address, and if you've moved since the foreclosure, they may never find you. Surplus held by a lender's trustee is even less visible: there is no public county list, and the trustee's law firm is not going to advertise that your money is waiting.

Texas Surplus Funds FAQ

How long do I have to claim surplus funds in Texas?

It depends on the type of sale. For a property tax sale, Texas Tax Code §34.04 gives you two years to file a claim. For a lender (trustee) foreclosure, that two-year deadline does not apply, and the trustee holds the surplus until the rightful owner or heirs claim it.

My home was foreclosed more than two years ago. Is it too late?

Not necessarily. If it was a lender foreclosure rather than a tax sale, the surplus may still be recoverable from the trustee, or from the Texas Comptroller if it was later reported as unclaimed property. A free case evaluation can tell you where the money is.

How do I know if my home was sold at a tax sale or by my lender?

A tax sale follows a lawsuit for unpaid property taxes and is conducted by a constable or sheriff. A lender foreclosure follows missed mortgage payments and is conducted by a trustee named in your deed of trust. Your foreclosure notices or the county property records will show which one it was.

Can heirs claim surplus funds if the former owner has passed away?

Yes. The heirs or the estate can claim the surplus, usually with probate or heirship documents. Trustees may also require the heirs to sign indemnity agreements before releasing the funds.

Texas Surplus Funds Glossary

Surplus Funds / Excess Proceeds
Money remaining after a foreclosure or tax sale pays all outstanding debts. Legally owed to the former property owner or their heirs, after any junior liens are paid.
Texas Tax Code §34.04
The statute governing claims for excess proceeds from a property tax sale. Establishes the 2-year filing deadline. It does not govern lender foreclosures.
Constable's Sale / Sheriff's Sale
A court-ordered auction of property for unpaid taxes, conducted at the county courthouse on the first Tuesday of the month.
Trustee's Sale
A non-judicial foreclosure auction conducted by the trustee or substitute trustee named in a deed of trust, after a borrower defaults on a mortgage or home-equity loan.
Trustee / Substitute Trustee
The person or firm, often the lender's law firm, that conducts a trustee's sale and holds and distributes any surplus.
Indemnity Agreement
A signed, often notarized, agreement a trustee may require before releasing surplus, in which the claimants agree to cover the trustee if another person later claims the same funds.
Odyssey Portal
The court case management system used by many Texas counties to look up tax sale cases and surplus deposits.
Petition for Excess Proceeds
The formal filing required to claim tax sale surplus from the court that ordered the sale.
Junior Lien
A lien with lower priority than the foreclosing debt (e.g., a second mortgage or judgment). Junior liens are paid from surplus before the former owner receives anything.
Unclaimed Property (Texas Comptroller)
Money reported to the state after it goes unclaimed for a set period. The state holds it for the owner, and it can be claimed without a deadline.
